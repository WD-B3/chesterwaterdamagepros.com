The West End is a residential part of Chester. The city's Vision 2035 comprehensive plan says the West End neighborhood covers most of Planning District 5 and part of District 4, and it names the Highland Avenue Station, the Union Square neighborhood, Memorial Park and the Chester Charter Scholars Academy as places within it.

If water is spreading through a West End house right now, call the dispatch line. A dispatcher asks where the water started, how far it has traveled and whether anyone is near electrical equipment, then explains the first steps while a crew is arranged.

What the city says about housing here

The plan reports that several new homeownership developments have recently been built in the West End, including the Union Square development at Third and Yarnall Streets. The city's housing page also describes Wellington Heights, a twin home project at the former Highland Gardens area and at Sixth and Reaney Streets, built for first-time buyers.

The plan also recommends targeting West End properties for blight removal and home repair assistance. These facts describe the area. They do not show that West End homes leak more or less than homes elsewhere, and we do not claim they do.

Twin and row houses: water does not stop at the wall

Twin houses share a wall with a neighbor, and row houses share two. When a supply line fails or a drain overflows, water can pass through a shared wall, a shared floor structure or a shared basement wall into the next home.

  • Tell the neighbor in person and in writing, and keep a dated note of what you said.
  • Photograph the wet side of the shared wall from your own home only.
  • Do not open a neighbor's wall or enter their home without permission.
  • Ask each household to report the loss to its own insurer.

Drying a shared wall works best when both sides are checked with a moisture meter, so ask the dispatcher how that can be arranged.

Newer homes still leak

A recently built home has few worn parts, but water damage does not need old plumbing. Supply lines to a washer or dishwasher, a water heater connection, an upstairs toilet and a roof flashing detail can all fail at any age.

If the home was built within a builder's warranty period, ask whether the warranty covers the failed part before repairs begin. Take photos first, and keep the failed part if you can.

The first hour in a West End home

  • Stop the sourceClose the fixture valve or the main. If you cannot reach it safely, say so on the call.
  • Stay clear of electricityDo not touch outlets, panels or appliances that are wet, and do not stand in water to reach a breaker.
  • Protect belongingsLift furniture legs onto blocks and move papers and electronics to a dry room.
  • Photograph firstThe Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises pictures or video of damage before cleanup.

Contaminated water from a drain or toilet is a different job. Keep people and pets away from it and say so when you call.

How extraction and drying work in a shared-wall home

Extraction removes standing water with pumps and extractors. Drying then pulls moisture from drywall, plaster, subfloor and framing using air movers and dehumidifiers, with readings logged each day. Read the water extraction page for the first stage and the structural drying page for the second.

In a twin or row house, equipment placement is part of the plan, because a wet party wall has two faces. A good scope lists which rooms get equipment and which readings show the wall is dry.

Insurance paperwork after a West End loss

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners insurance, while gradual leaks and flooding often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and report the loss promptly.

Keep photos, moisture readings, receipts and a written scope. If you rent, tell the landlord in writing and ask who carries the building policy. For a burst line, the burst pipe water damage page explains the sequence.

West End, Chester: what we could verify and what we could not

We have not verified FEMA flood-zone status for the West End as a whole. Zones follow the map, not the neighborhood name, so search your own street on FEMA's online map and ask the insurer whether a flood claim would be covered, since standard homeowners policies often exclude it.

If a backup seems to come from the street sewer instead of your own drain, say so when you call, and read the sewage backup cleanup page before touching anything. Wastewater is not something to clean up with household towels.

Questions from West End homeowners

Where exactly is the West End?

The city's plan describes the West End as covering most of Planning District 5 and part of District 4. We describe it by landmarks such as Highland Avenue Station, Union Square and Memorial Park, because we have not found official street boundaries.

Is my West End home in a flood zone?

That depends on the address, and neighborhood-wide status is unverified here. Look up the street on FEMA's flood map and ask your insurer what flood coverage you hold.

Water came through the wall from my neighbor's side of our twin. What do I do?

Stop what you can on your side, photograph the damage, tell your neighbor and report the loss to your insurer. Both sides of the wall should be checked with a moisture meter.

Do you serve every part of the West End?

Call with your address and the dispatcher confirms coverage. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and scope and price are explained before work starts.

My newer home has a leak. Should I call the builder first?

If a warranty may apply, ask the builder or warranty company what they need before repairs. Stop the water and photograph the damage first, then call dispatch to start drying so the damage does not grow.
