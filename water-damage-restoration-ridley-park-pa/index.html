Ridley Park's water problems follow the seasons, so this page is organized by season. Start with the one that matches what is happening now.

Winter: freezing nights and split pipes

Many houses in Ridley Park are Victorian-era homes built for Philadelphians who used the area as a summer resort. Those houses were not always built for winter, and pipes in porches, additions and exterior walls can freeze. A split often shows only after the thaw.

Open the faucet, thaw gently and shut the main if you see water. The frozen pipe water damage page lists safe steps. Never use open flame on a pipe.

When water is spreading in Ridley Park

A burst pipe or failed appliance in a house with a basement sends water down. A leak in an upstairs bathroom sends it through ceilings. Whatever the source, the first minutes matter.

  • Close the valveShut the fixture stop or the main. Open a low faucet to drain pressure.
  • Move out of the waterKeep children and pets out of any water that may touch outlets.
  • Lift what you canFurniture legs on blocks, papers and electronics up high.
  • Call the 24/7 lineTell dispatch your address and the water source.

Spring and fall: storm water and Crum Creek

A dam across Crum Creek created a 20-acre lake in Ridley Park by 1872, and the creek still marks the area's low ground. Heavy rain in spring and fall can overwhelm gutters, window wells and drains. Check your address against the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask your insurer whether flood damage is covered. Standard homeowners policies often exclude it.

Wind-driven rain through roofs and windows is covered on the storm water damage cleanup page. Leaves clog gutters in autumn and send water behind siding, so clearing them before storms is simple prevention.

Summer: air conditioners working overtime

Summer humidity makes air conditioners run long hours, and condensate lines clog. Many Victorian houses have attic air handlers or added units, and an overflowing pan drips into the ceiling below. The AC leak water damage page explains what to do and how the damage is dried.

Hot humid weather also helps mold. Wet materials that stay damp for more than a day or two should be checked, as described on the mold remediation page.

Getting here: I-95, Chester Pike and the rail line

Interstate 95 exit 8 serves Ridley Park directly, and Chester Pike, U.S. Route 13, connects the borough with Chester and Philadelphia. SEPTA's Wilmington/Newark line stops at Ridley Park station. Those routes keep the borough close to the Chester dispatch line.

Boeing's Vertical Lift Division headquarters is a large employer near the area, and light industrial and office buildings nearby need fast response when a roof drain or sprinkler fails. See commercial water damage restoration.

After the water is out in a Victorian-era house

Older houses often have plaster walls over wood lath, original wood floors and trim with many layers of paint. Plaster holds water longer than drywall and can crumble when it is soaked. Wood floors may cup after a few days if moisture sits under them, so early readings matter.

  • Plaster that is soft or bulging after a leak usually has to come off, and lath behind it needs to dry.
  • Wood floors are dried in place when possible, with attention to the subfloor underneath.
  • Painted trim is removed and reset carefully so it can be reused.
  • Attic and wall cavities from old additions should be checked for hidden moisture.

Ask the crew to explain why a material is staying or going, and to show the meter numbers behind the decision. The wall and drywall water damage page covers the repair side.
