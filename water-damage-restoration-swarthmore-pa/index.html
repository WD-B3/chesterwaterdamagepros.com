Swarthmore is a borough of about 6,500 residents in east-central Delaware County, with PA 320 running north to south through the center. If water is spreading in a Swarthmore house or rental right now, call the Chester dispatch line. It answers around the clock.

The borough was incorporated in 1893 and much of its housing dates from the Victorian period through the 1920s. Old houses age in particular ways, and the problems below start with how water behaves in them.

The problem in century-old houses: leaks that run unseen

The common water problem in houses built a century ago is the slow leak. A joint in an old supply line seeps, a cast-iron drain weeps, or a flashing fails above a bay window. Water follows framing, feeds on wood and plaster, and shows up late.

The first sign is often a smell or a soft spot instead of a puddle. A baseboard that lifts, a floorboard that cups or paint that blisters all deserve a moisture reading. See hardwood floor water damage repair and wall and drywall water damage repair for how those materials respond to water.

Professional drying in older houses follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Crum Creek, the campus and the borough edge

Crum Creek forms the western boundary of the borough. Swarthmore College, founded in 1864, covers about a third of the borough's land, and the Scott Arboretum is part of the campus. Properties near the creek should check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and your insurer can say whether flood damage is covered.

The college, the Swarthmore Public Library on Park Avenue and the Park Avenue Community Center are larger buildings with their own maintenance staff. Rentals and shared houses near campus need a clear chain of calls. Tenants should tell the landlord in writing, and landlords should keep dispatch on their emergency list.

Getting around: PA 320, the train and the 109 bus

Swarthmore Station on the SEPTA Media/Wawa line sits between the college and the town center, and SEPTA's Route 109 bus runs along PA 320 to Chester. Commuters leave houses empty on weekdays, and a leak that starts at 9 a.m. can run until evening. A cheap leak sensor near the washer, water heater and under the kitchen sink is worth considering.

Water that has run all day soaks more material. The longer a leak runs, the more the structural drying page applies, with multiple days of equipment.
