About Chester Water Damage Pros

A dispatch line for water damage restoration in Chester and nearby Delaware County towns.

☎ (856) 702-0892

Chester Water Damage Pros is a dispatch line for people with water in their homes and businesses. When you call, a dispatcher listens to what is happening, explains the first steps and arranges for licensed and insured restoration professionals to respond when a crew is available.

We keep the site simple on purpose. There are no forms to fill in and no waiting for an email. Every page leads to the phone, because a wet floor is not the time for paperwork.

How the dispatch line works

The first call is a short conversation. The dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, how deep it is, whether the source is stopped and whether anyone is near electrical equipment. Then you get a plan for the next steps, whether or not a crew has arrived yet.

  • Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, every day.
  • Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.
  • Scope and price are explained before work starts.
  • The professionals who respond are licensed and insured.

If you are wondering what to do when you call, the dispatcher walks you through it, and the pages that follow explain each step in more detail.

Standards we point people to

The industry publishes standards for this work. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. We encourage every homeowner to ask any company, including ours, whether its crews work to them.

Good restoration is measured. Ask for moisture readings, photos and an equipment log, and expect drying to be finished when the numbers say so.

What we do not promise

We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, weather and crew availability change. We do not promise an insurance outcome, because that is between you and your insurer. We do not promise that mold will never return, because moisture decides that.

We would rather tell you plainly what to expect than say something that sounds better and turns out wrong.

What a good first hour looks like

A good first hour is mostly about stopping damage from growing. The water source is shut off or isolated, standing water is removed, belongings are lifted off wet surfaces and the damage is photographed. Only then does the plan for drying and repair take shape.

The dispatcher can talk you through most of this over the phone, even before anyone arrives. If you can safely close a valve, move a few items and take pictures, you have already done work that helps both the drying and any insurance claim that follows.

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main if it is safe.
  • Stay clear of outlets, panels and appliances that are wet.
  • Photograph the source, the water and the damaged items.
  • Write down when you found it and what you did.
  • Call the dispatch line and describe what you see.

How we think about trust

A dispatch line asks you to trust a voice on the phone at a stressful moment, so we try to earn it with plain statements. We say what we know and what we do not. When a question belongs to your insurer, your city or a physician, we say so and point you there.

We also encourage you to check any company, including ours, against the published standards. Ask for moisture readings, ask for a written scope and ask how changes will be explained. Those questions protect you whoever you hire.

Local sources we rely on

Local facts on this site come from public agencies. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows mapped flood zones by address. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department publishes guidance for homeowners after a loss. Chester Water Authority lists an emergency line for water system problems, and the City of Chester Licenses and Inspections office posts permit and contractor forms.

If a page here cannot point to a primary source for a local claim, it does not make the claim.

Who the pages are written for

The pages on this site are written for homeowners, renters, landlords and small business owners who have water in a building and need to decide what to do next. They are not written for other contractors, and they do not assume you know the vocabulary.

Where a technical term is useful, such as water category or dry standard, the page explains it in plain words. Where a topic belongs to someone else, such as an electrician or a physician, the page says so.

What we cover

The services page lists every kind of water loss we dispatch for, from burst pipes and flood damage to sewage backups and commercial losses. Mold work is available as a secondary service when moisture has already led to growth.
