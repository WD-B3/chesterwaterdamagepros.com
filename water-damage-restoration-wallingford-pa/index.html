Wallingford is an unincorporated community in Nether Providence Township, Delaware County, with its own ZIP code, 19086, and a history that goes back to 1687. If water is spreading in a Wallingford home right now, call the Chester dispatch line. It answers 24 hours a day, and Chester is the next community to the south.

Wallingford is known for large, older houses, historic estates and a village center around the rail station. That mix of old buildings and long-lived materials decides how water damage should be handled here.

Neighborhood first: the village, the line and the creek

Wallingford sits north of Chester and about nine miles from Philadelphia. SEPTA's Media/Wawa line stops at a station designed by architect Frank Furness. PA 252, South Providence Road, marks the western edge, Interstate 476 runs to the east, and Crum Creek forms the township's border with Swarthmore.

Those boundaries matter for water. Properties near Crum Creek should be checked against the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and your insurer can say whether flood damage is covered, which standard homeowners policies often exclude. When storm water enters from outside, the storm water damage cleanup page applies.

In a Wallingford home, what to do first is simple: shut the valve, lift belongings off wet floors and describe the damage to dispatch. The drying that follows is guided by ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard.

When water is spreading in Wallingford

A big older house gives water more places to hide. A supply line that fails on the second floor can run behind plaster, into a wall cavity and down to a first-floor ceiling before a stain shows. Large attics and long runs of pipe add distance between a leak and where you notice it.

  • Shut the mainClose the main valve first, then open a low faucet to drain pressure from the lines.
  • Protect plaster and floorsLift rugs and furniture so wet plaster and wood do not stain.
  • Keep power safeSwitch off circuits only if the panel is dry. Water in a fixture means call 911 or the utility.
  • Photograph firstTake wide and close photos before cleanup, for any insurance conversation.

Estates, plaster and old floors

Wallingford has several listed historic properties, including the Thomas Leiper Estate, Westlawn and Wolley Stille, and many other houses of similar age. Plaster and lath walls and ceilings hold water longer than drywall and can crumble when wet. They are usually dried slowly and patched instead of torn out.

Old floors need the same care. The hardwood floor water damage repair page explains cupping and how drying is done without damaging the boards. For plaster and walls, see wall and drywall water damage repair, and for slow drying in thick old walls, structural drying.

Libraries, churches and schools

The Helen Kate Furness Free Library, founded in 1902, several churches and the Wallingford-Swarthmore School District buildings are part of the village. Institutional buildings often have large roofs, old drains and valuable collections. For a water event in such a building, see commercial water damage restoration, and call dispatch before anyone moves books or records.

Congregation Ohev Shalom stands at the corner of Routes 252 and 320. Any building that hosts regular gatherings should keep the facilities contact on its emergency list.

Questions from Wallingford homeowners

Do you cover every ZIP code in Wallingford?

Wallingford is ZIP code 19086, which includes the village around the rail station, the PA 252 corridor and the Providence Road side of Nether Providence Township. The Chester boundary on the south side is ZIP code 19013, and both are within our service area.

Is emergency water removal available in Wallingford?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24 hours a day, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available. Give the dispatcher your address and where the water is coming from.

Can plaster walls be dried instead of replaced?

Often yes, if the plaster is firm and the water was clean. The crew checks moisture readings and may drill small vent holes to dry the cavity. Crumbling or soft plaster is repaired or replaced.

Should I check a historic home for special rules before repairs?

Ask your township building office and your insurer before permanent repairs on a listed or historic property, since rules and coverage can differ.
