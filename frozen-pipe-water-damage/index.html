Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Chester, PA

The flood usually starts when the ice melts. Know what to shut off and what never to do with a frozen line.

☎ (856) 702-0892

A frozen pipe does its damage in two stages. Ice expands inside the line and can split the pipe wall, and the leak only shows once the ice melts and water flows again. That is why a quiet cold night can turn into a flooded floor the next afternoon. If water is already coming through a ceiling or wall in Chester, this is an emergency and dispatch is open 24 hours a day.

Frozen pipe water damage restoration in Chester, PA covers the cleanup after a thaw: stopping the water, extracting it, drying the wet cavity and rebuilding what cannot be saved. It does not replace the plumber who repairs the split.

What to do when a pipe is frozen or just thawed

Delaware County winters bring freezing nights, and lines in unheated spaces are the usual victims. Typical spots are pipes on exterior walls, garages, crawl spaces, unfinished basements and outdoor hose bibs. If a faucet runs slowly or not at all in cold weather, a frozen section is likely.

  • Open the faucetLeave the tap open so melting water has somewhere to go and pressure does not build behind the ice.
  • Thaw gentlyA hair dryer or warm towels work. Never use a torch, kerosene heater or any open flame. Start at the faucet end and work toward the blockage.
  • Know the mainIf you see a split or a wet spot appears, close the main shutoff right away.
  • Do not chase the leakIf water is already flowing inside a wall, shut the main, then call dispatch. Do not cut into the wall yourself.

Keep power off to any wet area you cannot reach safely. Water near a panel, outlets or ceiling fixtures is a reason to call 911 or the electric utility before you call us.

After a thaw, drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Why the damage is bigger than the split

The split in a frozen line is often small, a slit a few inches long. Once water pressure returns, that slit can spray steadily. Water finds the path of least resistance along framing and wire holes, so a break in an exterior wall can wet a ceiling and a floor on more than one level.

Because the cause is cold, the wet area is also cold. Cold surfaces dry slowly, so heat and dehumidification are part of the plan. Crews use moisture meters to confirm what is wet and thermal imaging to see wet areas behind paint.

How cleanup after a thaw works

First the water is shut off and extracted. Then insulation that soaked up water is removed, because wet insulation holds moisture against framing. Baseboards and a strip of the lowest drywall may be cut out to let air reach the cavity, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry targets.

The burst pipe page explains the supply-line side of the same problem. The standing water itself falls under water extraction, and wet cavities are dried as described on the structural drying page.

Cost factors and insurance

These factors move the price of a frozen pipe cleanup the most.

  • How long water ran. A split found in minutes is a small job. One that ran all day while no one was home is not.
  • Which levels got wet. Ceilings below an exterior wall break often add rooms to the scope.
  • Insulation and drywall removed. Cutting out wet material adds rebuild work but speeds drying.
  • Outdoor temperature. Cold slows drying and can mean more equipment days.

Whether freeze damage is covered depends on the policy and on whether heat was maintained in the home. Read the policy or ask your insurer. Photograph the damage before cleanup, keep receipts for emergency work, and wait for your insurer to agree before permanent repairs. Common questions

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Chester?

Yes. Freeze-related breaks often surface at night or on weekends when the thaw starts, and dispatch answers 24 hours a day. Tell the dispatcher where the water is and whether the main is shut. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Can I thaw the pipe myself?

You can try a hair dryer or warm towels with the faucet open. Never use open flame. If you see a crack or any water, shut the main instead and call for help.

Will a frozen pipe always burst?

No. Some pipes freeze and thaw without splitting, and others crack without leaking until the thaw. Check for wet drywall and damp insulation even when nothing seems to be running.

Does homeowners insurance cover frozen pipes?

Many policies cover sudden freeze damage but have conditions about keeping the home heated. Read your policy or ask your insurer before deciding.

Who fixes the pipe?

A plumber replaces or repairs the pipe. We remove the water, dry the structure and handle restoration, and can coordinate timing around your plumber.
