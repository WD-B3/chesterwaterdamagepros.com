Stop an appliance leak right now

  • Close the supply valveWashers and dishwashers have shutoff valves behind or beneath them. A refrigerator line usually has a small valve near the wall.
  • Unplug if safePull the plug only if the outlet and floor are dry. Otherwise cut the breaker if the panel is reachable and dry.
  • Pull the machine out carefullyDo not drag a heavy unit through water, because that can break more lines.
  • Take photographsShow the appliance, the hose and the wet floor.

Before anything else, here is what to do: stop the supply, keep clear of wet outlets and photograph the damage. Professional drying of the floor and wall base follows ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Common appliance leak sources

Washing machine supply hoses are the best known. A braided hose can burst under pressure, and rubber hoses harden and crack. Dishwasher drain lines and door seals leak slowly. Refrigerator ice-maker lines are thin plastic tubes that can split behind the unit.

Garbage disposals leak from their sink flange and drain connection into the cabinet below. Many appliance leaks are slow, which means the water has time to soak the subfloor, the base of cabinets and drywall behind the machine.

What cleanup involves

The crew extracts surface water, then checks the floor, the wall behind the unit and the cabinet base with a moisture meter. Vinyl and laminate flooring often trap water underneath, so thermal imaging helps find damp spots. Wet baseboards, a section of drywall or flooring may need to be removed so the structure can dry.

Drying is described in detail under structural drying. A leak that reaches cabinets and the sink area is handled on the kitchen water damage page. If the leak ran for days under flooring, ask about hardwood floor repair or carpet replacement.

Prevention, briefly

Replace rubber washer hoses with braided hoses every few years, and check for bulges at the fittings. Turn off the washer supply valves when you travel. Check behind the refrigerator when you move it for cleaning. A cheap water sensor under a dishwasher costs far less than a floor.

Finding a leak you cannot see

Appliance leaks are often found by accident: a soft spot in the floor in front of the washer, a ceiling stain under the laundry room, or a musty smell behind the refrigerator. If you suspect one, pull the machine forward and look at the floor, the wall and the valve for rust, mineral crust and water marks. Run a cycle while watching the hose connections, since pressure is what exposes a weak fitting.

Check the date on braided hoses if they are printed. Hoses that are many years old, or that bulge near the fittings, are worth replacing before they fail. Washer valves that are stiff or weeping should be replaced by a plumber instead of forced.

If you rent in Chester, tell your landlord in writing right away and keep dated photos. Responsibility for an appliance leak depends on the lease and on who owns the machine. The City of Chester posts rental registration and licensing forms on its website, so landlords can check what applies to the unit.

Cost factors and insurance

  • Run time. A leak caught in an hour is small, a slow leak that ran for weeks is not.
  • Flooring. Vinyl, laminate and engineered wood over a subfloor often must come up.
  • Adjacent rooms. Water that runs under a wall affects the next room.
  • Cabinets. Water in a base cabinet may cross into kitchen scope.

Common questions

Yes. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day, every day. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available. Shut the supply valve first and tell the dispatcher what you see.

Who repairs the appliance?

An appliance technician or plumber repairs the machine or the hose. We handle the water, the drying and the damage to floors and walls.

How far does washer water travel?

It follows the floor slope and soaks flooring seams, baseboards and the wall base. Basement laundry water can also reach a ceiling or finished walls below.

Can I dry it with a fan?

A fan helps surface moisture but cannot dry subfloor or wall cavities. Check with a moisture meter before assuming it is dry.

Is the water category different for a dishwasher?

Dishwasher or washer discharge is often gray water, which means more caution about disposal and protective equipment.
